Communic-Aid · design plan, section 6

How Communic-Aid learns

Two small learners watch what each person does and adjust to them. Nobody is ever asked to rate anything: everything is read from behaviour.

The dwell learner sets how long a person has to look at an everyday button (tiers 0 and 1: navigation and typing). The signal chooser decides, for risky buttons (tiers 2 and 3: sending, calling, deleting), whether to ask for a second signal and which one: blink and hold, raised eyebrows, saying "yes", or the switch.

1Reading what happened

Every look and every request for a signal ends in one of four outcomes. An outcome is only decided once its time window has closed, so the learner never learns from a guess.

A look at an everyday button

OutcomeHow the app knows
workedThe button fired and was not undone within 5 s. For a letter, the next key was not Backspace within 3 s.
accidentIt fired, then was undone within 5 s, or a letter was followed straight away by Backspace.
gave upThe person looked away before the fill finished, then came back to the same button within 2 s. They meant it, but the wait felt too long.
passed byThey looked away and did not come back. A stray glance; the app notes how long it lasted.

A request for a second signal

OutcomeHow the app knows
workedThe right signal came in time and the action was not undone.
accidentThe signal came, then the action was undone within 5 s.
gave upIt timed out, but the person tried: a wrong signal, a half blink, a late signal, or a look back within 3 s.
blocked an accidentIt timed out with no attempt and no return. The look was probably a mistake, and the signal stopped it.

Undo, then Redo, counts as worked: the undo was the mistake, not the action.

2The situation

The same person needs different settings when tired or when their gaze is shaky. Two yes or no questions give four groups, and each learner keeps separate counts for each group.

  • Tired means 20 minutes into the session, after 6 pm, or 2 or more accidents or give-ups in the last 6 tries.
  • Shaky means gaze wobbles more than 1.5 times as much as it did during calibration.
  • A group with little data of its own borrows the totals of all groups, so a new situation does not start from nothing.

3The dwell learner

It learns two habits of the person, then picks the dwell that balances them against how costly a mistake is on that tier.

  • Stray glances: how long do looks that were not meant usually last? If they are short, a shorter dwell is safe.
  • Patience: how long will the person wait before looking away? If not long, a long dwell causes give-ups.

Both habits come from six running counts per group, updated by simple addition after each outcome. To choose, the learner draws plausible values for the two habits (a wide spread when it has seen little, a narrow one later; this is called Thompson sampling) and scores every dwell from 800 to 2000 ms:

accident chance A(d) = e^(-d / stray glance) give-up chance G(d) = 1 - e^(-d / patience) score(d) = meant × [ (1 - G) × (1 - 0.6 × d in seconds) - 0.5 × G ] - not meant × A × accident cost

In words: a look that worked is worth 1, minus a little for each second waited. A give-up costs 0.5. An accident costs the tier's accident cost.

Staying calm

A dwell that jumps around feels random and stressful, so it moves slowly and only after real selections.

  • The first 20 selections in a group stay at 1500 ms. Stray glances never count towards this.
  • After that, each selection moves the dwell 5% towards the learner's pick, but at most 10 ms shorter or up to 100 ms longer. Accidents are fixed quickly; speed comes slowly.
  • It never goes below 800 ms or above 2000 ms.

Risky buttons (tiers 2 and 3) keep a fixed 1000 ms fill before asking for a signal. The undo strip is fixed at 800 ms.

4The signal chooser

For Send, React and End call (tier 2) it can choose dwell only or a signal. For Call, Delete and Forward (tier 3) a signal is always required, and it only chooses which.

First, rule out what is not allowed

  • Signals the person switched off in Settings, or that failed their signal check.
  • Blink and eyebrows while tracking is poor (the camera cannot see them reliably).
  • Dwell only on tier 3.

Then, score what is left

For each group, tier and option it keeps a tally of the four outcomes. It draws a plausible set of chances from each tally (again, wide when the tally is small), multiplies by what each outcome is worth, and asks for the option with the best score.

A worked example

Rekha deletes a message in the evening. After about 10 tries of each, her tallies look like this:

Her blinks often fail when she is tired, so eyebrows win clearly. The random draw still picks blink now and then, so if her blinks get better, the chooser will notice. In the morning she is in a different group with its own tallies, where blink may win.

5Settings you tune

All of these live in config.toml, and the costs can also be changed from the study panel during a pilot.

SettingNowWhat it does
Accident cost, tiers 0 to 31, 2, 4, 8How much a mistake hurts on each tier. Higher means slower, safer choices.
Give-up value-0.5How much waiting too long hurts.
Blocked accident value0.15 × costHow much credit a signal gets for stopping a mistake.
Time penalty0.6 per sHow much each second of dwell costs on a look that worked.
Starting dwell and warm-up1500 ms, 20A careful start before learning moves anything.
Step down, step up10 ms, 100 msHow fast the dwell may shorten or grow per selection.
Dwell limits800 to 2000 msNever shorter or longer than this.

Changing a cost never wipes what was learned. The learners store only counts of what happened; costs are applied at the moment of choosing, so a new value takes effect on the very next choice.

6Watching it live

While the core runs, the terminal prints one line per learning event:

CHOOSE call tier 3 tired+steady -> say "yes" scores: say "yes" +0.44 raise eyebrows -0.38 switch -0.82 OUTCOME open priya tier 0 worked reward +0.10 dwell 1500 ms DWELL tired+steady: warm-up 1 of 20 selections, staying at 1500 ms OUTCOME call tier 3 worked reward +0.53 check: say "yes" ROUTER tier 3 tired+steady say "yes": worked 1, gave up 0, blocked an accident 0, accident 0

The same events, with their rewards, are saved in data/<person>/events.jsonl for analysis after the study.
